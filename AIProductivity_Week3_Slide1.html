Industry Stop 1 · Market Analyst

The Analyst's Gather-and-Verify Loop

She used to lose the first day just collecting — reading reports, opening tab after tab to learn a new market. Now a Deep Research agent drafts a sourced landscape in an hour. But she doesn't ship what the machine wrote: she opens the citations, confirms the market-size figure traces to a real report, and reads the sources, not just the summary.

Her advantage isn't that AI gathers fast — everyone has that. It's verification speed: she can confirm every load-bearing fact faster than a colleague can finish reading.

Concept 1 of 4

Answer Engines with Citations

Search always meant a page of links you had to open yourself. Perplexity, ChatGPT search, Google's AI overviews, Claude — you ask in plain English and get a synthesized answer with numbered citations attached. It feels finished.

The citation is an invitation to verify, not a guarantee. For anything that matters, click it and confirm the source really says what the answer claims.
Plain English → sourced answer
You ask "What share of EU new-car sales were electric last year?"
Engine answers · then you verify "~14.6% of EU new registrations were BEVs. [1] [2] [3]" ✓ Click [1]: does the cited page actually state that figure — for that year, that region?
Concept 2 of 4

Deep Research Agents

Give an agent a topic — "the state of EV charging in Europe" — and instead of one answer it plans, browses dozens of sources, and returns a structured, multi-page report with a source list. What took an analyst two days now drafts in twenty minutes.

The catch: a longer, more confident report is more persuasive and just as capable of being wrong. Read it like an editor — never let polish substitute for proof.

Concept 3 of 4

Research on Your Own Documents

Load a stack of PDFs, transcripts, or reports into a tool like NotebookLM and ask questions across all of them at once — "what did every one of these interviews say about pricing?" — grounded in your own material, with a pointer back to the exact passage.

It answers from what you gave it — but can still misread or over-generalize. Click through to the source paragraph and confirm it read the passage the way you would.

Concept 4 of 4

Verification & Honest Citing

Every AI research tool hands you claims and citations. The discipline: treat each load-bearing fact as unconfirmed until you've opened the source and seen it — and cite honestly, crediting real sources rather than pasting AI text as your own thought.

Skip verification and you don't save time — you launder a hallucination into a report someone makes a decision on.

The Honest Slide

Where AI Research Bites

The slide most courses skip. Anyone who says AI removes the need to check your sources is setting you up to be the next cautionary headline.

⚖️ The signature real-world case: multiple lawyers have been sanctioned by courts for filing briefs full of AI-generated cases that were completely fabricated — discovered only when a judge tried to look them up.
Fabricated citations

AI invents a study, a case, or a quote that looks perfectly real, formatted flawlessly, and simply does not exist.

Hallucinated facts

A plausible statistic stated with total confidence and no real source behind it.

Over-trust & ethics

Leaning on AI text without reading it slides into plagiarism — passing a machine's draft off as your own understanding.

AI removes the drudgery of gathering. The responsibility for what's true stays exactly where it always was — with you.
