Chapter One · 8:40 a.m.

The AI Office Landscape — Meet the Tools, Pick the Right Helper

Rule one of AI in the Office: there is no single "AI button." There are several very different helpers — free Copilot on the web, Copilot Pro, Microsoft 365 Copilot, Gemini for Workspace, and standalone ChatGPT/Claude — each with a licence, a privacy trade-off, and a sweet spot. Before touching the inbox, Sam matches the job to the right tool — and learns what is safe to paste where.

📖 The story so far Monday, 8:40 a.m. Sam opens the laptop for the first time in a week and the coffee goes cold. 200 unread emails. A red thread from a client named Rivera Freight titled "Still waiting — this is unacceptable." Three missed meetings sitting as recordings in Teams. And a calendar reminder: "Status report to Director Okafor — due 12:00 noon." Before panicking, Sam does the professional thing: figures out which AI helper fits each job — and which is even allowed to see company data. Click each tool to inspect it, then commit.
🟢
Free · web chat

Copilot (free)

The free Microsoft Copilot chat on the web or in the sidebar. Great for general drafting, rewriting and questions — but it does not read your actual emails, files or meetings, and free-tier chats aren't covered by your org's data promise.

🔵
Paid · personal

Copilot Pro

A paid personal subscription. Adds Copilot inside personal Word, Excel, PowerPoint and Outlook.com, plus priority access. Powerful for individuals — but it is not the enterprise licence that safely reaches your work tenant's data.

🏢
Paid · work tenant

Microsoft 365 Copilot

The enterprise licence, grounded in your work emails, files, chats and meetings via Microsoft Graph — inside Word, Outlook and Teams. This is the one that can summarise Sam's real inbox. Your org must buy and enable it; data stays in the tenant.

🟦
If you're on Google

Gemini for Workspace

Google's enterprise AI inside Gmail, Docs, Sheets and Meet. Same idea as M365 Copilot, different suite. Excellent — only if your company runs on Google Workspace, not Microsoft 365.

Sam's meeting catch-up checklist

  • Open the  Recap  for each missed meeting — read the AI summary, not the full transcript

  • Extract  action items with owner + due date  into a clean list
  • Spot the  ownerless action  AI left unassigned and assign it before it's lost
  • Verify one recap claim against the transcript — AI can mis-attribute who said what
  • Send a short  follow-up  with the action list so nobody's waiting on Sam

Lesson 1

The AI Office Landscape

Match the tool to the job: free Copilot (general chat, no work data), Copilot Pro (paid personal), Microsoft 365 Copilot (enterprise, grounded in your tenant), Gemini for Workspace (Google's version). And know what's safe to paste where. Chapter 1.

Lesson 2

AI in Word

Turn messy notes into a polished one-pager, re-tone it for any audience — and always catch the invented statistic or fake citation. AI drafts prose; you supply the true numbers and sources. Chapter 2's "27.4%" is the whole point.

Lesson 3

AI in Outlook

Summarize a 200-email inbox, triage to the few that matter, draft a tricky reply in the right tone, and turn an email into a meeting — while catching hallucinated commitments and wrong recipients before you hit send. Chapter 3.

Lesson 4

AI for Meetings & Notes

Catch up on missed meetings from a recap, extract action items with owner + due date, assign the ownerless ones, send a follow-up — and handle recording consent correctly. Summaries save time; you verify and own the actions. Chapter 4.

Northwind Logistics is a teaching scenario. Real AI tools, licences, privacy rules and recording laws change — always confirm what your workplace and local law allow before pasting company data into an AI or recording a meeting, and remember the golden rule this whole week was built on: AI is leverage, not truth. A human who reads the output ships the work.
