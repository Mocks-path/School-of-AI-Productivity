Building Decks with AI — describe the talk, let AI draft the slides

Four hands-on activities. Match each starting point to the right generation method, assemble a strong deck prompt block by block, run a rewrite clinic that turns three wordy slides into tight ones, and drill the story-first habits that separate a real presentation from an AI bullet-dump.

Progress
1

Match the starting point to the right way in

AI tools (Copilot in PowerPoint, Gemini in Slides, Gamma, and chat assistants) can build a deck three ways: from a prompt, from a document, or from an outline. Each fits a different starting point. Study the three below, then drag each situation into the method that fits it best. Guess freely — wrong answers explain themselves on hover.

P

From a prompt

"I have an idea in my head, no draft." You describe audience, goal and topic; AI invents the structure and first-draft content.

  • Fastest way from a blank page to a rough deck
  • Best when you're still shaping the idea
  • Needs the most editing — AI guesses the specifics
D

From a document

"I already wrote it up." Point AI at a report, memo, or notes and ask it to turn that source into slides.

  • Best when the facts already live in a file
  • Keeps you anchored to real, verifiable content
  • You still trim — a 5-page memo is not 5 slides
O

From an outline

"I know the flow, not the words." You hand AI a slide-by-slide outline; it expands each line into a drafted slide.

  • Best when you already own the narrative arc
  • You control order; AI fills in phrasing
  • Most predictable — least likely to wander off-topic

The rewrite clinic — turn three wordy slides tight

AI's biggest deck sin is wordiness: it happily dumps a paragraph onto every slide. Your job is one idea per slide, tight text, and a story the audience can follow. Each patient below shows a bloated AI-generated slide. Pick the single best fix. Every choice explains itself, so wrong guesses teach too.

Title: Our Q3 Results
Body: In the third quarter of this fiscal year, our team achieved a number of important milestones across several key areas including revenue, which grew, as well as customer retention, which also improved, and we onboarded new clients too.

The story-first quiz — draft fast, then make it yours

AI writes a deck that looks finished. Looking finished is not the same as being ready to present. This quiz drills the habits that turn a generic first draft into a talk that lands: one idea per slide, a real narrative, speaker notes, and honest fact-checking. Five situations, one honest answer each — the misconceptions are where the real learning is.

Quick reflect — make it stick

A sentence each is enough. Writing it in your own words is what moves it into memory. (Nothing is saved or sent.)

You have a 4-page project update memo and need a 6-slide deck for your leadership team by tomorrow. Write the prompt you'd give AI — name the audience, goal, length, and source — and one thing you'd check in the draft before trusting it.
AI hands you a clean, confident 10-slide draft. It reads well. Describe the exact pass you'd make before presenting it — what you'd cut, what you'd verify, and what you'd add.
