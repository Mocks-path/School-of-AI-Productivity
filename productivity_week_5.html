Chapter One · The AI PM Landscape

Pick Your Stack — And Decide What AI Owns vs. What You Own

Rule one of AI project management: the AI is your project coordinator, not your project lead. There's no single "PM AI" — there are tools built into the suite you already use, each with a plan tier and a sweet spot. Today Priya matches the tool to her team, then draws the line between what AI drafts and what she commits to.

📖 The story so far Monday, 9:00 a.m. The VP forwards Priya one line: "Own the newsletter launch. First issue live in six weeks. You've got Sam (design), Aisha (copy), and Dev (email platform). Go." No plan, no tool, no template. Before Priya panics, she does the professional thing: she picks the PM stack her team will actually use, and decides which decisions she'll never delegate to AI. Chapter Three · Tracking, Status & Communication

A Blocker Appears — Now Communicate Like a Pro

Week 4. A blocker hits. This is the real test of a project lead: not that nothing went wrong, but how honestly and clearly you handle it. Today Priya uses AI to draft two very different status updates, turn a messy standup into tasks — and catch a "watermelon" status that's green on the outside, red on the inside.

📖 The story so far Thursday of week 4. Dev messages: "The email platform's API changed — the send integration is broken. Might be 3 days to fix." That's on the critical path. Priya's instinct isn't to hide it; it's to communicate it well. The VP needs a one-line, decision-ready summary. The team needs specifics and next steps. Same truth, two audiences. And she has to make sure nobody's status is quietly lying.
🚨 The Blocker — Critical path at risk
Dev's update: "Email send integration broken — the platform changed its API. Estimated 3 days to fix. This blocks the build & test task, which the launch date depends on."

Priya asks AI: "Turn this blocker into a status update — one for the VP, one for the team." Same facts, two very different drafts. Toggle between them, then judge whether the framing is right.

🍉 Catch the Watermelon — green on top, red inside

Priya's dashboard shows the "Build & Test Email" task as 🟢 GREEN — on track. But she knows Dev is blocked by the API break with no vendor ETA. A status that reports green while the work is actually stuck is a watermelon — and it's how projects fail on the day everyone thought they were fine.

  • Choosing which trade-off to make on the blocker
  • Owning the bad news — you deliver it, not the bot

Chapter Four · Automation & Capstone

Automate the Busywork — Then Assemble the Whole Course

Priya keeps getting the same request: "Where are we?" A pro automates the recurring ask — with a trigger → action flow and guardrails, so the automation never lies on her behalf. Then the capstone setup: the five modules that will carry launch day.

📖 The story so far Week 5. Every Friday the VP, Legal, and two stakeholders each ping "status?" separately. Priya answers the same thing four times. So she asks AI to design an automation: a trigger that fires every Friday, pulls task status from the board, drafts a status summary, and sends it — but pauses for her review before anything goes out. Automation removes the busywork; the guardrail keeps her name honest. Build the flow, then set up the capstone.
🎯 Checkpoint · The golden rule of PM automation
AI can auto-generate and auto-send the weekly status. Should Priya let it send with no human step?
⏰

Every Friday, 2 p.m.

Gather + draft

🛡️
Guardrail
Priya reviews

It pings Priya to approve/edit before anything sends. Human in the loop.

Why the guardrail matters: If the board had a stale "green" left over from Chapter 3's watermelon, a fully-automatic send would have blasted a wrong status to the VP with Priya's name on it. The review step is one click — and it's the difference between automation that helps and automation that embarrasses you. Automate the busywork; never automate the accountability.
