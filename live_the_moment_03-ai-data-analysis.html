AI Data Analysis & Insights — turn a spreadsheet into an answer

A messy grid of numbers isn't an insight — it's raw material. Four hands-on drills: run the five-step analysis pipeline, sharpen a vague question into one AI can actually answer, assign fields to build a PivotTable, match each question to the right chart, then test whether you can catch AI when it quietly misreads your data.

The analysis pipeline — five steps, every time

AI can crunch a spreadsheet in seconds, but "give me insights from this file" usually returns confident-sounding noise. Real analysis follows an order. Get the order right and both you and the AI produce answers you can trust. Skip a step — especially cleaning or validating — and you'll ship a wrong number to your boss with a straight face.

STEP 1
Clean
Fix blanks, stray text in number columns, inconsistent dates and duplicate rows. Dirty data poisons every step after it.
→
STEP 2
Structure as a Table
Turn the range into an Excel Table (Ctrl+T). Named columns, no gaps — this is what lets AI and PivotTables read it reliably.
→
STEP 3
Ask
Pose one specific, answerable question — not "any insights?" A sharp question is half the analysis.
→
STEP 4
Visualize
Pick the chart that fits the question — comparison, trend, share or relationship — so the answer is readable at a glance.
→
STEP 5
Validate
Sanity-check the numbers by hand. AI can misread a column or a date and never tell you. Trust, then verify.

Vague vs specific — pick the question AI can actually answer

The quality of your answer is capped by the quality of your question. A vague prompt makes AI guess what you meant — which column, which time frame, which metric — and it guesses wrong silently. Build the PivotTable — assign each field to its shelf

A PivotTable summarises thousands of rows into a small answer, and it has exactly four shelves. The question we're answering: "What was total revenue per Region, split by Quarter, for online orders only?" Drag each of the six fields onto the shelf where it belongs — Rows (what you list down the side), Columns (what you split across the top), Values (the number that gets summed), or Filters (what you narrow the whole table to). Match the question to the right chart

A chart is an argument. The wrong shape hides the answer or, worse, implies one that isn't there. Four question-types map to four chart families: Trend over time → Line, Compare categories → Bar/Column, Part of a whole → Pie/Stacked (only a few slices), Relationship between two numbers → Scatter. Trust, then verify — catch the AI before it embarrasses you

AI reads your spreadsheet fast, but it doesn't understand it. It can sum the wrong column, treat text-formatted dates as random labels, or double-count a merged row — and it reports the result with total confidence. It also matters where your data goes: pasting a customer list into a public chatbot is a privacy decision, not just a convenience. Answer these four. Each locks in with the reasoning.

✓

The 30-second sanity check before you send it on

Before any AI-produced number leaves your screen, run it past these. Most AI analysis errors are caught in under a minute — if you actually look.

  • Σ
    Do the totals add up? Sum one column yourself with a quick =SUM(). If the AI's grand total doesn't match, it summed the wrong range or dropped rows.
  • 📅
    Did it read the dates as dates? Text like "01/02/2025" can be read as Jan 2 or Feb 1 — or as plain text that won't group by month at all. Check that a "monthly" trend has the right number of months.
  • ↔
    Did it use the column you meant? "Revenue" and "Units" sit side by side. AI can grab the neighbour. Spot-check one row against the source.
  • ?
    Does the answer pass the smell test? A region with 3% of orders can't be 60% of revenue unless something's off. If a number surprises you, that's a prompt to dig — not to forward.
