AI Design & Visuals — make it look like it belongs to you

AI can fill a deck with images, icons and charts in seconds — and just as fast make it look generic, off-brand or quietly dishonest. Four hands-on drills: learn the three visual principles that carry every good slide, build a slide on the right zones, catch what's wrong with an off-brand AI image, judge whether a chart is honest, then test your instinct for when a visual is helping and when it's lying.

Three principles that carry every slide

A good-looking slide isn't about decoration — it's about guiding the eye to the one thing that matters. AI tools like PowerPoint Designer and Copilot can lay out slides and drop in visuals for you, but they don't know your priorities. If you understand these three principles, you can steer the AI and override it when it makes the wrong call. Skip them, and you'll ship a slide that's busy, off-brand, or says nothing at a glance.

PRINCIPLE 1
Hierarchy
One clear focal point per slide. Size, colour and position tell the eye what to read first. If everything shouts, nothing lands.
→
PRINCIPLE 2
Whitespace
Empty space isn't wasted — it's what makes the important thing readable. Crowded slides exhaust the audience.
→
PRINCIPLE 3
Less text, more visual
Slides support you speaking; they aren't the script. One idea, one image or chart, a few words. The detail goes in your mouth, not the slide.
→
THE GLUE
On-brand consistency
A theme, template and brand kit lock your fonts and colours so every AI-generated slide looks like the same deck — not a ransom note.

Keeping AI output on-brand — themes, templates, brand kits

The single biggest reason AI slides look "off" is inconsistency: three fonts, four blues, icons in five different styles. The fix is to set the rules once and let every AI-added element inherit them.

  • T
    Theme sets your global fonts and colour palette. Pick or build one first, before Designer touches a slide — then AI layouts snap to your fonts and colours automatically instead of guessing.
  • ▦
    Template defines your slide layouts — title, section header, content, comparison — so structure stays consistent whether you or the AI builds the next slide.
  • ◆
    Brand kit (logo, exact hex colours, approved fonts) is your source of truth. Many AI tools let you upload it so generated visuals match. If yours doesn't, apply the theme colours by hand.
  • ✎
    Designer & Copilot help — you decide. They suggest layouts and pick images fast. Accept when the suggestion strengthens hierarchy; override when it buries your focal point, adds a decorative image with no meaning, or drifts off your palette. The AI optimises for "tidy," not "your point."

Judgement calls — prompting, rights & honest charts

AI visuals raise questions a spell-checker never will: is this image licensed for a client deck? Does this chart type flatter the data or distort it? Should there even be an image here? Answer these four. Each locks in with the reasoning.

✓

The 30-second visual check before you present it

Before any AI-generated slide, image or chart goes in front of an audience, run it past these. Most visual mistakes are caught in under a minute — if you actually look.

  • ◎
    Is there one clear focal point? Squint at the slide. The first thing your eye finds should be the thing you want the audience to remember. If nothing jumps out — or three things do — hierarchy is broken.
  • ◈
    Does it look like your deck? Same fonts, same colours, same icon style as the slides around it. A stray AI image in a different palette screams "pasted in." Match it to your theme or cut it.
  • 📊
    Is the chart honest? Check the y-axis starts at zero (unless there's a good, labelled reason), that the chart type fits the data, and that the numbers on the chart match your source. AI can pick a flattering shape you didn't ask for.
  • ✂
    Does every image earn its place? A decorative photo that adds no meaning is just clutter competing with your point. If you can delete an image and lose nothing, delete it.

Quick reflect — make it stick

A sentence each is enough. Writing it in your own words is what makes it stay. (Nothing is saved or sent.)

You ask AI for an image for a slide about "customer trust." It gives you a generic stock-style handshake. Would you use it? What would you do instead, and why?
Copilot made a bar chart of your monthly sales but started the y-axis at 90 instead of 0, making a tiny rise look huge. What's the problem, and how do you fix it?
