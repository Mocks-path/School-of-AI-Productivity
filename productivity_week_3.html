Chapter One · 9:00 a.m.

The AI Research Landscape — One Job, Many Tools

Rule one of AI research: a plain chatbot is not a search engine. There's a whole toolkit — chatbots, answer engines, Deep Research agents, notebook tools, and academic search — and each is built for a different part of the job. Before Ana types a single query, she matches the tool to the task, and learns exactly why a plain chatbot will happily invent a citation.

📖 The story so far 9:00 a.m. Ana's director drops the brief on her desk: "Plant-based snacks — is the category worth entering? Credible, sourced, on my desk by five. And I follow the footnotes." Ana's first instinct is to open a chatbot and ask it to "research the market and give me sources." She stops. She remembers a colleague who did exactly that — and got five beautiful, perfectly formatted citations, two of which pointed to studies that never existed. Before touching a keyboard, Ana maps the toolkit. Click each tool to inspect it, then commit.

Draft · never source

Plain Chatbot

ChatGPT / Claude / Gemini without live search. Brilliant at outlining, rewording and brainstorming — but it predicts plausible text, so it can invent citations that look perfect and don't exist.

🔎
Sourced answers

Answer Engine

Perplexity, ChatGPT Search, Gemini with search. Searches the live web and answers with inline links you can click. Your fast, cited starting point — but you still open the links.

🧭
Multi-step agent

Deep Research

An agent that runs dozens of searches over minutes and returns a long, cited report. Great for breadth on a big question — slower, and every claim still needs a human to trace it back.

📓
Grounded in YOUR docs

NotebookLM

Upload your own sources; it answers only from them, with citations back to the exact passage. Perfect for synthesis once you've gathered trustworthy documents.

🎓
Peer-reviewed lens

Elicit / Consensus

Search engines over real academic papers. They pull findings from peer-reviewed studies with real DOIs — the gold standard when a claim needs scientific weight.

Chapter Two · 10:30 a.m.

Smart Search & Information Gathering — Ask Well, Then Trace

A weak prompt returns a vague, hard-to-verify blob. A strong research prompt names the question, the scope, the sources you'll accept, and the format. Today Ana writes prompts that earn checkable answers, runs an answer engine, and — the crucial habit — follows every citation back to the primary source, catching a confident answer that's simply wrong.

📖 The story so far 10:30 a.m. Ana opens Perplexity and starts firing off prompts. The first is lazy — "tell me about plant-based snacks" — and gets a Wikipedia-flavoured summary she can't cite. So she tightens it: a clear question, a time window, a request for primary sources and numbers. The answers get sharper. Then one answer engine reply states a market size with total confidence — and links to a press release quoting a blog quoting nobody. Ana traces the chain. Chapter Three · 1:00 p.m.

Synthesis & Analysis — Turn a Pile of Sources into a Brief

Gathered sources are just a pile until you find the themes. Today Ana loads her vetted documents into NotebookLM, builds a synthesis matrix — who says what, where they agree, where they clash — and drafts the brief. And the crucial beat: she catches the AI smoothing over a real disagreement into a fake "consensus," and dropping a caveat that changes the meaning.

📖 The story so far 1:00 p.m. Ana has six vetted sources — market reports, a peer-reviewed consumer study, two competitor filings. She uploads them to NotebookLM, which will only answer from those documents and cites the exact passage each time. She asks it to summarise where sources agree and disagree. The draft reads beautifully — maybe too beautifully. One line claims "all sources agree the category is booming," but Ana remembers Source C warned that growth is slowing and hinges on price. AI loves a clean story; reality is messier. Build the matrix, then catch the flattened caveat.

Chapter Four · 3:00 p.m.

Verification, Citations & Ethics — Make It Boss-Proof

A draft is not a brief until every claim survives a source check. Today Ana fact-checks the brief line by line, tests each of five citations (two are fabricated), fixes the attribution, and adds an honest AI-use disclosure — so the work is credible and ethical. She tests before she trusts, always.

📖 The story so far 3:00 p.m. The brief drafts fast, and the reference list looks immaculate — five clean citations, perfect formatting. That polish is exactly the trap. Ana's colleague once shipped a report with two AI-invented sources and got it bounced. So Ana does the unglamorous work: she opens each citation and asks two questions — does this source exist, and does it actually say what we claim? Then she adds a short disclosure of how AI was used. Lesson 1

The AI Research Landscape

Match the tool to the job: plain chatbots draft (and can invent citations), answer engines cite live links, Deep Research goes wide, NotebookLM grounds in your docs, Elicit/Consensus search real papers. Chapter 1.

Lesson 2

Smart Search & Gathering

Strong prompts name the question, scope, sources and format. Run an answer engine or Deep Research, then follow every citation back to a primary source — confidence is not correctness. Chapter 2.

Lesson 3

Synthesis & Analysis

Load vetted sources into NotebookLM, build a theme matrix, draft the brief — and refuse the false consensus. A dropped caveat or a flattened disagreement is the error that matters. Chapter 3.

Lesson 4

Verification, Citations & Ethics

Test every citation twice: does it exist, does it back the claim? Fix attribution, remove fabrications, and disclose how AI helped. Boss-proof means source-proof. Chapter 4.

Nourish & Co. is a teaching scenario. Real AI research tools, their web access and their limits change constantly — always confirm what a tool can actually see and cite, and remember the golden rule this whole week was built on: AI is leverage, not truth.
