Chapter One · 1:30 p.m.

The AI Presentation Landscape — Meet the Tools, Pick the Right One

Rule one of AI decks: there's no single "make me a presentation" button. There are four very different helpers, each with a licence, a workflow, and a sweet spot. And there are two ways to start: prompt-to-deck (describe it) or document-to-deck (feed it your notes). Leo has notes — so his path is almost decided already.

📖 The story so far 1:30 p.m. Leo opens Q3_notes_ROUGH.docx and his stomach drops. Three pages of fragments: "revenue up," "churn issue?", "new region did well," a pasted table nobody formatted, and a line reading "growth ~40%?? (check!!)". His manager pings: "Board's at 3. You've got the Q3 review. Thanks!" Before he panics, he does the professional thing: he figures out which AI tool fits the job — and whether to describe a deck from scratch or convert the notes he already has. Click each tool to inspect it, then commit.
🟧
Paid licence

Copilot in PowerPoint

Microsoft 365 Copilot, built into PowerPoint. Can turn a Word doc into a first-draft deck, apply your company template, and draft speaker notes — inside the file you already use. Powerful, but a paid add-on IT must enable.

✨
AI-native · web

Gamma

AI-first tool that generates a whole deck from a prompt or pasted notes in seconds, with slick auto-layouts. Free tier + paid. Great for speed and story — but check whether pasting company data to a web tool is allowed.

🎨
Design-led

Canva (Magic Design)

Design-first, with Magic Design generating decks from a prompt and a huge template/brand-kit library. Strongest when visuals and brand polish matter most. Free tier + paid teams for locked brand kits.

🟦
If you're on Google

Google Slides + Gemini

Google Workspace's AI inside Slides — drafts slides, images and text from a prompt. Same idea as Copilot, different suite. Great only if your org lives in Google, not Microsoft.

🧱 Rescue the wall-of-text slide — AI restructures in seconds

Leo asks AI: "This slide has one dense paragraph. Restructure it into a headline + 3 short supporting points, and suggest a simple visual." Instant restructure — but he still reads it to confirm the meaning survived the trim.

💔 Before — wall of text
  • One 60-word paragraph explaining churn, causes, timing and next steps all at once
  • 10pt font to make it fit → nobody past row two can read it
  • No headline → the audience doesn't know what to take away
💚 After — headline + 3 points
  • Headline: "Mid-market churn is the one thing to watch in Q4"
  • Rose 2 pts in the mid-market tier → concentrated, not company-wide
  • Improved late in the quarter → the fix is already working; here's the plan

Leo's pre-flight checklist

  •  Edit pass  — consistent fonts, one idea per slide, every number verified against source
  • Refine  speaker notes  into my own voice — key phrases, not full paragraphs
  • Run  Speaker Coach  — check pace, filler words, and monotone
  • Fix  accessibility  — alt text on every image, contrast that passes
  • Pre-flight: title slide, page numbers, no typos, fonts embedded, backup PDF
  • Practise the open + close once out loud; time it under the slot

Lesson 1

The AI Presentation Landscape

Four tools, four trade-offs: Copilot in PowerPoint (paid, in-app), Gamma (AI-native web), Canva Magic Design (design-led), Google Slides + Gemini. And two starts — prompt-to-deck vs document-to-deck. Match the tool to the job. Chapter 1.

Lesson 2

Building Decks with AI

Generate a draft from your notes with a strong, structured prompt; fix the bullet-dump into one idea per slide; generate speaker notes — and catch the invented stat before it reaches a board. Chapter 2.

Lesson 3

AI Design & Visuals

Apply the brand theme, re-prompt an off-brand image, turn a table into an honest chart (and reject the misleading truncated-axis version), and rescue a wall-of-text slide. Chapter 3's honesty check is the whole point.

Lesson 4

Polish, Delivery & Practice

The edit pass, speaker notes in your own voice, Speaker Coach for pace and fillers, accessibility (alt text + contrast), a pre-flight checklist — then practise and walk in confident. Chapter 4.

Leo's quarterly review is a teaching scenario. Real AI tools, licences and privacy rules change — always confirm what your workplace allows before pasting company data into a web tool, and remember the golden rule this whole week was built on: AI drafts fast; you direct and verify. A human who reads every slide presents the truth.
