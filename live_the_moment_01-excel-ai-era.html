What "AI in Excel" actually means today

"Excel has AI now" is a headline, not a single button. In 2026 there are really four different doors into AI-assisted spreadsheets, and they differ on one thing that matters most to you: cost and where your data goes. Study the four cards, then in the drag activity below you'll match real tasks to the right door.

C

Microsoft 365 Copilot

A paid add-on that lives inside Excel. Ask it in plain English; it writes formulas, summarizes, and suggests charts. Works best on data formatted as an Excel Table.

+ Never leaves your Excel file + Understands your actual sheet − Extra paid licence on top of 365 − Not on the free/web-only tiers
AD

Analyze Data

A free button already in Excel (Home tab). Click your Table and it auto-suggests trends, charts, and pivot summaries — no typing, no AI account.

+ Free, built in, no setup + Great first look at any dataset − Suggestions only; can't write formulas − No open-ended "ask a question"
CB

External chatbots

ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini in a browser. You paste or upload data (or just describe it) and copy the answer back. Powerful, but your data leaves Excel.

+ Free tiers; extremely flexible + Best at explaining & drafting formulas − Data leaves your machine — privacy risk − Can't see your live sheet; you copy back
G

Google Sheets + Gemini

Google's spreadsheet with Gemini built into the side panel. Ask it to build formulas, tables, or summaries — the Sheets equivalent of Copilot.

+ Works right inside Sheets + Good if your team lives in Google − Needs the right Google/Workspace plan − Different formulas than Excel
💡 The one rule that ties them together: tools inside your app (Copilot, Analyze Data, Sheets+Gemini) keep data where it is. External chatbots are the most flexible but your data leaves the building — so you decide what's safe to share. Activity 3 drills exactly this.

The "4 places AI helps" model

Whatever door you use, AI helps a spreadsheet in exactly four ways. Learn these four verbs and you'll always know what to ask for. This is the mental map for the whole week.

01

Understand data

"What's in this sheet? Any duplicates, blanks, or weird outliers?" AI reads your columns and describes what you actually have before you analyze it.

02

Write formulas

Describe the result in words — "average sales per region, ignoring blanks" — and AI drafts the =AVERAGEIF(...) for you to paste and verify.

03

Analyze

"Which month grew fastest? Summarize the top 3 customers." AI turns rows of numbers into trends, comparisons, and a plain-English story.

04

Automate

Clean text, split columns, or draft a macro/Office Script for a repetitive task you do every week — so next month it's one click.

Spot the privacy risk — safe to paste, or not?

The moment you paste data into an external chatbot, it leaves your computer and may be stored or used to improve the model. So before you paste, ask: "Would I be comfortable if this ended up outside my company?" Judge each scenario — click Safe or Risky for instant feedback. Answer all six for your score.

💡 The safe habit: before pasting into ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini, anonymize — replace names, IDs, and emails with "Customer 1, 2, 3", or paste only the columns you need. Better still, for sensitive data use a tool inside your app (Copilot, Analyze Data) so nothing leaves. Many chatbots also have a setting to turn off training on your chats.

Do it now — and then check AI's work

AI is fast and confident — and sometimes confidently wrong. It can invent a function that doesn't exist, mix up which column is which, or give a formula that runs but computes the wrong thing. Here's a real drafted formula. Your job isn't to write it — it's to verify it, the single most important AI-Excel skill.

You asked a chatbot:

your prompt"In my sales Table, give me the total revenue for the West region only. Region is in column B, Revenue is in column D."

It replied with this formula. What should you do before trusting it?

AI's formula=SUMIF(B:B, "West", D:D)
