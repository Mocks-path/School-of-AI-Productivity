What "AI for slides" means — and the tool map

"AI makes your slides now" is a headline hiding six very different tools. Some live inside PowerPoint; some are standalone web apps that invent a whole deck from a sentence. They differ on four things that decide which one is right for a job: how you feed it (a prompt vs your own document), how editable the result is, how cleanly it exports to .pptx, and cost. Study the six cards, then match jobs to tools in Activity 2.

Co

Copilot in PowerPoint

Microsoft's AI inside PowerPoint. Type "create a 10-slide deck on Q3 results" or point it at a Word doc, and it drafts real editable slides in your file.

+ Native .pptx — fully editable, no export + Can build from your own Word doc − Paid M365 Copilot add-on required − Layouts can be plain / text-heavy
Mode: prompt or document
Best for: Office users, business decks
De

PowerPoint Designer

A free feature in PowerPoint (Design tab → Design Ideas). It doesn't write content — you add your text/images and it suggests polished layouts.

+ Free, built in, no AI account + Instantly upgrades ugly slides − Styles your slides, won't write them − Needs you to supply the content first
Mode: layout assist
Best for: polishing existing slides
Ga

Gamma

A standalone app that turns a prompt or pasted text into a full designed deck in seconds. Slides are "cards" you edit in the browser.

+ Fastest zero-to-draft from a prompt + Generous free tier to try − .pptx export can lose fidelity − Its card format ≠ true PowerPoint
Mode: prompt or document
Best for: fast first drafts, web decks
Ba

Beautiful.ai

Slides built on "smart templates" that auto-arrange as you add content — everything stays on-brand and aligned by design rules.

+ Always tidy, consistent layouts + Great for teams needing brand polish − Paid; limited free trial − Less freeform than PowerPoint
Mode: prompt + smart templates
Best for: polished business/brand decks
Ca

Canva (Magic Design)

Canva's AI drafts a deck from a prompt, then you edit in Canva's easy drag-drop editor with a huge library of graphics and templates.

+ Beautiful visuals, easy to edit + Free tier; friendly for non-designers − .pptx export imperfect vs native − Can feel template-y / on-trend
Mode: prompt
Best for: visual, social, marketing decks
Gs

Google Slides + Gemini

Google's slides with Gemini in the side panel — draft slides, generate images, and rewrite text right where your team already works.

+ Lives inside Google Slides + Good if your org uses Workspace − Needs the right Workspace/Gemini plan − Not native .pptx (export to convert)
Mode: prompt + in-app assist
Best for: Google-first teams
💡 The one line that sorts them all: if you need a true, editable .pptx to hand around your Office org, tools inside PowerPoint (Copilot, Designer) win. If you want the fastest good-looking draft from a sentence, standalone generators (Gamma, Canva, Beautiful.ai) win — just budget time to fix the .pptx export. Activity 3 drills exactly this prompt-vs-document choice.

How a deck generator actually works

Every AI slide tool, however different it looks, runs the same four-stage pipeline under the hood. Knowing it tells you where to steer — because the earliest stage, the outline, decides everything downstream. This is the mental map for the whole week.

STAGE 01

Outline

From your prompt or document, AI writes a list of slide titles — the story's skeleton. Fix the story here and every slide below inherits it.

STAGE 02

Slides

Each outline title becomes a slide with bullet content — headline plus a few supporting points drafted from the source.

STAGE 03

Layout

The tool picks a layout per slide — title-only, two-column, image + text, chart — arranging the content so it reads cleanly.

STAGE 04

Theme

A consistent look — fonts, colours, spacing, and often generated images — is applied across all slides at once.

prompt / document → OUTLINE → SLIDES → LAYOUT → THEME → editable deck
💡 Steer early: most tools show you the outline first and let you edit it before generating slides. Two minutes fixing titles there beats twenty minutes re-writing finished slides. AI drafts, you direct the story.

Prompt-to-deck, or document-to-deck?

Every generator can start two ways. Prompt-to-deck: you type a topic and AI invents the content from scratch — fast, but it may guess or generalize. Document-to-deck: you feed it your own report, notes, or transcript and AI reshapes what you already wrote — grounded and accurate, because the facts come from you. Read each scenario and pick the smarter starting mode. Answer all six for your score.

💡 The rule of thumb: when you already have the facts written down (a report, notes, a spec), document-to-deck keeps AI honest — it summarizes your real content instead of inventing. Use prompt-to-deck for brainstorming, templates, and topics where a generic first draft is fine to reshape. Either way, you still direct the outline.

Do it now — then check AI's deck

AI slide tools are fast and confident — and their defaults are often generic, wordy, and sometimes factually wrong. A generated deck may invent a statistic that sounds real, cram every bullet with text, or list a competitor that doesn't exist.
