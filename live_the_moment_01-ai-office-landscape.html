What "AI in the Office suite" actually means

"Your Office has AI now" is a headline hiding a menu. In 2026 there are several different products with confusingly similar names, and they differ on the two things that matter most: what it costs and what data it can reach. Study the five cards — the divide to remember is public tools that only see what you paste versus work tools that can securely read your own files and email.

C

Copilot (free)

Microsoft's free chatbot at copilot.microsoft.com or in Windows/Edge. A general assistant for drafting, answering, and web questions — a lot like ChatGPT.

Cost: Free · Sees: only what you type/paste
+ Free, no setup, good for general drafting − Cannot see your work files or email − Treat like any public chatbot for privacy
CP

Copilot Pro

A personal paid upgrade (~US$20/mo). Adds Copilot buttons inside the consumer Word, Excel, PowerPoint and Outlook apps for individuals and families.

Cost: ~$20/mo · Sees: files you open in the app
+ Copilot inside the desktop apps + Priority access, better for individuals − Not the same as the work/enterprise version
M

Microsoft 365 Copilot

The work version, licensed by your organization (~US$30/user/mo). It's built into Word, Outlook, Teams, Excel and more, and can securely read your company files and email.

Cost: ~$30/user/mo · Sees: your tenant's files & email
+ Grounds on your real work content + Data stays inside your company tenant − Only if your org buys the licence
G

Gemini for Workspace

Google's answer, built into Docs, Gmail, Sheets and Meet. Same idea as M365 Copilot for teams whose work lives in Google Workspace.

Cost: Workspace plan · Sees: your Workspace files
+ AI right inside Gmail & Docs + Data stays in your Workspace account − Needs the right Google Workspace plan
X

ChatGPT / Claude

Independent chatbots in a browser. Superb at drafting, explaining and reasoning — but they only know what you paste and your data leaves your machine.

Cost: Free / ~$20 · Sees: only what you paste
+ Free tiers; extremely flexible & strong − No access to your files unless you upload − Data leaves your machine — privacy risk
💡 The one line that sorts them all: "Can it read my own files and email securely?" Only the work tools (M365 Copilot, Gemini for Workspace) can — and they keep that data inside your organization. Everything else only knows what you personally hand it. Activity 3 drills exactly what's safe to hand over.

Where Copilot lives — and how it "grounds" on your work

M365 Copilot isn't one window — it shows up inside the app you're already using. What makes the work version powerful is grounding: instead of guessing, it looks up your real files and email through Microsoft Graph, so its answers are about your content — and that content never leaves your company tenant.

WORD

In Word

Drafts a document from a prompt, rewrites a paragraph, or summarizes a long report. Can pull from files you reference with /.

OUTLOOK

In Outlook

Summarizes a long email thread, drafts a reply in your tone, and coaches your writing before you hit send.

TEAMS

In Teams

Takes meeting notes, lists action items, and answers "what did I miss?" — grounded on the live transcript.

CHAT

In Copilot Chat

"Find the deck Priya sent last week" — searches across your files, chats and mail via Graph to find and combine.

The AI-assisted workday — and what's safe to paste

Across a normal office day, AI helps in four repeating moments: Write (documents, drafts), Communicate (email, chat), Meet (notes, action items), and Find (search across your stuff). But the moment you use a public chatbot, whatever you paste leaves your machine and may be stored. Before you paste, ask: "Would I be fine if this ended up outside my company?" Judge each scenario — click Safe or Risky. Answer all six for your score.

WRITE

Write

Draft, rewrite and summarize documents in Word or Docs.

COMMUNICATE

Communicate

Summarize threads and draft replies in Outlook or Gmail.

MEET

Meet

Capture notes and action items live in Teams or Meet.

FIND

Find

Search across your files, chats and mail to locate answers.

💡 The safe habit: for anything with real names, salaries, IDs, or a "Confidential" label, use a work tool (M365 Copilot / Gemini for Workspace) that keeps data inside your tenant — not a public chatbot. If you must use a public tool, anonymize first (Customer 1, 2, 3…) or describe the shape of your data without the data itself. Many chatbots also let you turn off training on your chats.

Do it now — then check AI's work

Copilot is fast and confident — and sometimes confidently wrong. It can summarize a meeting and misattribute a decision, invent a "fact" that reads well, or draft an email with a wrong date. Even grounded on your files, it can misread them. Here's a real drafted summary. Your job isn't to write it — it's to verify it, the single most important AI-office skill.
