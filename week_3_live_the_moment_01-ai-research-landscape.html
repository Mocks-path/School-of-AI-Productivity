Progress
1

What "AI research" actually is — and the tool map

"AI can do my research" is a promise that's half true. Used well, AI turns a five-hour literature scan into thirty minutes of reading. Used blindly, it hands you confident, well-written answers that are simply wrong. The difference is understanding what each tool can and can't see — starting with what AI research really is.

✓ What it IS

  • A faster way to find, summarize, and organize sources you'll still read yourself
  • A drafting partner for questions, outlines, and comparisons
  • A shortcut to the right documents — that you then verify
  • Best when it shows you citations you can click and check

✕ What it ISN'T

  • A source of truth — it predicts likely text, it doesn't "know" facts
  • A replacement for reading the actual paper, law, or report
  • Reliable on very recent, niche, or specialist topics without live web access
  • Trustworthy just because the answer sounds authoritative

The tools differ on one thing that matters most: can it actually reach the live web and show you real, clickable sources — or is it answering from memory? Study the five cards, then Activity 2 has you match research tasks to the right one.

CB

Plain chatbot

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini answering from training memory. Fast and fluent — but no live web unless a search mode is on. Great for explaining, brainstorming, drafting.

+ Instant, flexible, free tiers + Best at explaining a concept − No live web = highest fabrication risk − Citations, if given, may be invented
AE

Answer engines

Perplexity and similar search the live web in real time, then write an answer with numbered links to the pages it used.

+ Live web + clickable citations + Great for current, factual questions − Can still misread or over-trust a source − You must open the links to confirm
DR

Deep Research agents

Multi-step agents (ChatGPT/Gemini/Perplexity "Deep Research") that run many searches over minutes and return a long, cited report.

+ Broad, structured, many sources + Best for a first landscape scan − Slower; can still cite weak sources − Long ≠ correct; still needs checking
NB

NotebookLM

Google's tool that answers only from documents you upload. It's grounded in your sources, with citations pointing back to your files.

+ Grounded in your own PDFs/notes + Very low fabrication — cites your text − Only knows what you give it − Not for open-web discovery
AC

Academic tools

Elicit & Consensus search real peer-reviewed papers, pull findings, and link to the actual studies — built for evidence, not chit-chat.

+ Real papers with DOIs / links + Best for scientific / evidence questions − Narrow to academic literature − Still read the paper, not just the summary
💡 The one rule that ties them together: a tool that reaches the live web or your own documents and shows you clickable sources (answer engines, Deep Research, NotebookLM, Elicit/Consensus) is far safer for facts than a plain chatbot answering from memory. When a tool can't point you to a real source, treat its "facts" as a draft to verify, not an answer.
2

Why AI hallucinates — and how grounding fixes it

A large language model doesn't look facts up; it predicts the next most likely words. That's why it writes so fluently — and why it can produce a citation that looks perfectly real (plausible author, journal, year, even a DOI) but doesn't exist. It's not lying; it's pattern-completing. Here's the mechanism, and the fix.

01

It predicts, not knows

An LLM generates text that's statistically likely, not text it has verified. Where facts are fuzzy, it fills the gap with something plausible-sounding.

02

Fake citations form

Asked for a source, a plain chatbot can assemble a realistic-looking reference from patterns — real-sounding author, title, and year that lead nowhere.

03

Grounding = give it sources

Grounding means the tool retrieves real documents first, then answers from them — answer engines, NotebookLM, Elicit all do this. Far less made-up.

04

You still verify

Grounding reduces fabrication; it doesn't eliminate misreads. A grounded tool can still cite a real page that doesn't actually say what the summary claims.

🏅

The Golden Rule of AI Research

Always verify against the primary source. AI points you to information faster than ever — but the moment a fact matters, open the original paper, page, law, or dataset and confirm it with your own eyes. Grounded tools make this quick; ungrounded ones make it non-negotiable. An AI answer is a lead to check, never the final word.
