Week 2 · Lesson 01 · The AI Office

The AI Office landscape.

Word, Outlook, Teams and PowerPoint are where most office work actually happens — and every one of them now has an AI assistant sitting inside it. But "AI in Office" is not one thing you switch on; it's a confusing family of products with wildly different prices, privacy rules and abilities. This lesson gives you the honest map: what these assistants really are, which one you actually have, where AI genuinely saves you time across the workday — and where it still hands you a confident, generic draft you have to fix.

01 / 04

Why the Office suite plus AI matters

You don't live in a spreadsheet all day. You write documents, answer email, sit in meetings and hunt for a file someone sent last month. The Office suite is the connective tissue of knowledge work — and that's exactly why putting AI inside it is a bigger deal than any single clever chatbot.

For decades the assistant in Office was passive: spellcheck, autocomplete, a paperclip nobody asked for. What's changed is that an assistant can now read your actual work — the report you're writing, the email thread you're replying to, the meeting you just left — and act on it in plain English. "Draft a reply", "summarise this thread", "turn these notes into slides." The knowledge gap that used to eat your afternoon was rarely about ideas; it was the grind of turning intent into a finished, well-formatted artifact.

"AI in Office" simply means closing that gap where you already work. The key word — and the whole reason enterprise AI feels different from a public chatbot — is grounding. A grounded assistant doesn't answer from the open internet alone; it answers using your files, email and meetings, while respecting who's allowed to see what. What "grounding" really buys you

Notice the difference in the two runs. The grounded assistant routes your request through Microsoft Graph — the permission-aware index of your organisation's files, mail and meetings — so the answer knows about your project, your client, your Tuesday meeting. Critically, it only ever sees what you already have permission to see, and the data stays inside your organisation's tenant. A public chatbot has none of that context, so it writes a plausible, polished paragraph about nothing in particular. That's the entire value proposition of AI-in-Office: relevance, earned by respecting permissions.

Five doors into AI-in-Office

"Copilot" is not one product, and that's the most expensive misunderstanding beginners make. There are at least five different offerings with the same friendly name-space, and they differ sharply on cost, what data they can touch, and privacy. Click each card to see exactly what it is, what it costs, and when to reach for it.

In plain terms: free Microsoft Copilot is a public chatbot with no access to your work files. Copilot Pro is a personal upgrade that adds AI buttons inside your home Office apps. Microsoft 365 Copilot is the enterprise version that grounds on your organisation's data via Graph. Google Gemini for Workspace is Google's equivalent inside Docs, Gmail and Meet. And ChatGPT / Claude are standalone assistants you paste work into. Same friendly promise, five very different doors.

The privacy line you should never forget

There's one dividing line across all five: does your work data stay inside your organisation's environment, or does it leave? Free Microsoft Copilot and public ChatGPT/Claude are consumer-grade — anything you paste travels to a third party, and you should never paste salaries, contracts, client lists or unreleased plans. Microsoft 365 Copilot and Gemini for Workspace keep your data inside the enterprise tenant, honour your existing file permissions, and (under commercial data protection) don't use your prompts to train public models. Before you lean on any of them for real work, learn which tier you're on — it's the difference between a convenience and a leak.

In each app it sees the context of that app — the document open in Word, the thread open in Outlook, the meeting live in Teams — and it reaches, through Microsoft Graph, into the rest of your work when it needs to. The pattern under all four

Read the four moments back to back and the shape is always the same: you bring intent and context, AI brings a fast first draft, and then you edit. That last step is not optional. The most productive AI-Office users aren't the ones who send whatever Copilot writes — they're the ones who use the minutes AI saves to sharpen the draft, add the one detail only they know, and make sure the meeting recap actually names the right owner.

Hype vs. reality: four claims, flipped

The internet is full of "AI runs your whole office now" promises. Before you lean on it, flip each card and read what the claim looks like once you've actually used these assistants on real work. Honest expectations now save you from a generic email — or a leaked file — later.

None of this means "don't use AI in Office." It means use it as leverage, not as an oracle. The realistic workflow is boring and reliable: ask for a draft, read what it wrote, fix the tone and the facts only you know, then send. AI makes each loop faster. It does not remove your responsibility for what goes out under your name.

Reality

Even grounded assistants hallucinate — they can misattribute a quote, invent a meeting action item, or cite a file that doesn't say what they claim. It always sounds confident. Read the draft against what you actually know before it goes out under your name.

Reality

AI's first draft is fast, fluent and generic — it doesn't know the client's history, the political sub-text, or the one number that matters. Its output is a strong starting point, not a finished product. Your edit is where the real value, and your voice, gets added.

Your getting-started path

Start small and safe. First, find out which assistant your organisation has actually licensed — free Copilot, Copilot Pro, Microsoft 365 Copilot or Gemini — because everything else depends on it. Second, pick one low-risk task: ask it to summarise a long email thread or turn a page of notes into bullet points. Third, practice the edit — never send the first draft; add the one thing only you know. Always, keep confidential work inside approved tools and read every draft before it leaves your hands. Do that ten times and AI becomes a genuine part of your workday.

What to carry forward

You now have the honest map of AI across the Office suite. The apps still matter; AI just lets you talk to your own work in plain English. The magic word is grounding — a good assistant answers from your files, mail and meetings through Microsoft Graph, respecting permissions and staying in your tenant. There are five doors (free Copilot, Copilot Pro, Microsoft 365 Copilot, Gemini for Workspace, ChatGPT/Claude) with real price and privacy differences, and AI helps at four moments: write, communicate, meet and find. It can be wrong and it can be generic, so you edit. Next lesson: writing and rewriting with Copilot in Word, step by step.
