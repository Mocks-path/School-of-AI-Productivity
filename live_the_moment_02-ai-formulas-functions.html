AI Formulas & Functions — describe the answer, let AI write the formula

Four hands-on activities. Match plain-English needs to the right formula family, assemble a formula prompt block by block, play error-clinic doctor on three broken formulas, and test the discipline that keeps you out of trouble — because a formula that runs is not the same as a formula that's correct.

1

Match the need to the right formula family

You don't need to memorize 400 functions — you need to recognize which family solves the job in front of you, then let AI write the exact syntax. Study the three families below, then drag each plain-English need into the family that answers it. Guess freely — wrong answers explain themselves on hover.

L

Lookup & retrieve

"Pull a value from another table by matching a key." The bread and butter of joining data.

  • XLOOKUP — modern, flexible, handles not-found
  • INDEX + MATCH — works in every version
  • Matches an ID / name / SKU to a row
A

Aggregate by condition

"Add up or count rows that meet one or more rules." Answers the "how much / how many" questions.

  • SUMIFS — total where criteria are met
  • COUNTIFS — count rows matching rules
  • AVERAGEIFS — mean within a segment
R

Reshape & clean

"Fix messy text or spill a filtered list." Turns raw exports into something usable.

  • TRIM / CLEAN — strip stray spaces
  • TEXTSPLIT — break one column into many
  • FILTER / UNIQUE / SORT — dynamic arrays

The verify-first quiz — trust, but test

AI writes formulas that run. Running is not the same as correct. This quiz drills the single most important habit in AI-assisted spreadsheets: check the output against a small range where you already know the answer. Five situations, one honest answer each — the misconceptions are where the real learning is.

Quick reflect — make it stick

A sentence each is enough. Writing it in your own words is what moves it into memory. (Nothing is saved or sent.)

You inherit a spreadsheet with a formula you don't understand: =INDEX(H:H,MATCH(1,(A:A=K2)*(B:B=L2),0)). Write the prompt you'd give AI to explain it in plain English — and what you'd check before trusting the explanation.
